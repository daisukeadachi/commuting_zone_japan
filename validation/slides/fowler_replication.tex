\begin{frame}{The delineation}
  For municipalities $i$ and $j$ with commuting flows $f_{ij}$ and resident labour forces $W_i$, the proportional flow is
  \[
    p_{ij} = \frac{f_{ij} + f_{ji}}{\min(W_i, W_j)}, \qquad p_{ij} \le 0.999 ,
  \]
  and the dissimilarity is $1 - p_{ij}$ with a zero diagonal.
  \vfill
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Average-linkage hierarchical clustering, cut at a fixed tree height of 0.977.
    \item Commuting matrix: residents mainly working, ordinary households, the only definition available for 2020.
    \item 1{,}678 municipalities: the four main islands and the Okinawa main island, plus every municipality joined to one by a permanent road link.
    \item Cores and urban areas for the fit statistics: the Urban Employment Area central cities and their areas.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Technical validation}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item The cutoff. It is inherited rather than derived. Does the similarity between delineations pick out a better value?
    \vfill
    \item Non-contiguity. Zones that hold a municipality sharing its zone with none of its neighbours.
    \vfill
    \item Split urban areas. Externally defined urban areas cut across more than one commuting zone.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{One departure from the existing pipeline}
  The existing Japanese pipeline joins the flow file to its own transpose in a way that keeps a pair only when the flow from $i$ to $j$ is recorded, so a pair connected only in the reverse direction enters as unconnected.
  \vfill
  \begin{itemize}
    \item The matrix it clusters is asymmetric; this replication uses the symmetric numerator the source method specifies.
    \item Between 1.2 and 4.4 percent of pairs are affected in each year, moving up to four zones and up to 108 municipalities.
    \item Measured year by year in \filelink{validation/notes/method\_crosscheck.md}.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}
